\section{Introduction}
Robust trajectory optimization is vital for autonomous robotic systems operating under uncertain dynamics and environmental disturbances. While successive convexification (SCvx) enables nonlinear trajectory optimization, nominal solutions may remain vulnerable to disturbances. We investigate a counterexample-guided robustification framework for OpenSCvx that integrates adversarial falsification with trajectory optimization. A falsifier identifies disturbances causing continuous-time safety violations, and those disturbances are explicitly converted into constraints for subsequent SCvx iterations. The initial experiment tests whether this feedback improves sampled obstacle clearance. Comparisons of computational cost against existing optimization methods and of explicit constraint updates against neural policy fine-tuning require separate experiments.
\section{State-of-the-Art}
\begingroup
\setlength{\parskip}{0.8em}
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}

Mutapcic and Boyd~\cite{mutapcic2009} developed an optimization method that repeatedly searches for worst-case scenarios and adds new constraints to prevent similar failures. Dawson and Fan~\cite{dawson2022} applied a related approach to trajectory planning where an optimizer learns from failures identified by a falsifier. Their method produced more robust trajectories while taking less than half the time of competing methods in their experiments.

Shi et al.~\cite{shi2024} used RL (PPO) to discover disturbances that could cause quadrupedal robots to fail. They then retrained the robot's neural controllers using these failures to improve their robustness.

Abdul et al.~\cite{abdul2025} developed a SCvx method that plans trajectories while accounting for disturbances within a predefined bounded set. Their approach also accounts for errors caused by approximating nonlinear dynamics. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2026} later accelerated this method using GPUs. Elango et al.~\cite{elango2025} developed a way for SCvx to enforce safety constraints throughout the entire trajectory rather than only at selected time steps.

Building on these works, we add a falsifier to OpenSCvx that actively searches
for disturbances that cause trajectory failures. Each discovered failure introduces a new scenario safety constraint and allows SCvx to replan. We investigate the resulting sampled clearance and computational cost.



\section{Limitations}
Existing robust SCvx methods~\cite{abdul2025,wang2026} account for bounded disturbances, but solving these optimization problems can be computationally heavy. Counterexample-guided methods~\cite{dawson2022} improve planning by learning from failures. Meanwhile, adversarial RL~\cite{shi2024} improves neural controllers through fine-tuning rather than explicit constraint updates. This experiment examines adaptive falsification with continuous-time SCvx; it does not establish that this combination is novel.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

We consider a planar unicycle with continuously actuated
forward and angular velocities. The robot moves without
lateral slip, and its dynamics are deterministic.
The planning horizon $\mathcal H=[0,T]$ is fixed.
Initial and terminal configurations are prescribed.

The state and control vectors are
\begin{equation}
\bm{x} =
\begin{bmatrix}p_x\\p_y\\\theta\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\bm{u} =
\begin{bmatrix}v\\\omega\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

The nonlinear dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\bm{f}(\bm{x},\bm{u}) =
\begin{bmatrix}
v\cos\theta\\
v\sin\theta\\
\omega
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Let $\bm{g}(\bm{x})\leq\bm{0}$ denote the
collision-avoidance constraints and
$\bm{G}(\bm{u})\leq\bm{0}$ the actuator limits.
The objective is to minimize the traveled distance.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Problem 1 (P1)}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\underset{\bm{x}(\cdot),\,\bm{u}(\cdot)}
{\operatorname{minimize}}
\quad & \int_0^T v(t)\,dt
\\[4pt]
\text{subject to}\quad &
\left|\;\;
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\bm{x}} &= \bm{f}(\bm{x},\bm{u})\\
\bm{g}(\bm{x}) &\leq \bm{0}
\\
\bm{G}(\bm{u}) &\leq\bm{0}
\\
\bm{x}(0) &= \bm{x}_{\mathrm{init}}
\\
\bm{x}(T) &= \bm{x}_{\mathrm{goal}}
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:unicycle_ocp}
\end{equation}

The actuator limits are defined by
\begin{equation}
\bm{G}(\bm{u}) =
\begin{bmatrix}
-v\\
v-v_{\max}\\
\omega-\omega_{\max}\\
-\omega-\omega_{\max}
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}


\subsection{Counterexample-Guided Robustification}

We extend Problem 1 with a bounded constant steering
disturbance $b\in\mathcal B=[-\bar b,\bar b]$:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bm{x}}_b =
\begin{bmatrix}
v\cos\theta_b\\
v\sin\theta_b\\
\omega+b
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Adversarial Falsification.}
For a circular obstacle with center $\bm c$ and
radius $R$, define the violation function
\begin{equation}
q(\bm{x}_b)=R^2-\|\bm p_b-\bm c\|_2^2.
\end{equation}

Given the current control $\bm u^j$, the falsifier
searches for the worst-case disturbance and time:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(b_j^*,t_j^*)\in
\underset{b\in\mathcal B,\ t\in[0,T]}
{\operatorname{argmax}}
q(\bm{x}_b(t;\bm u^j)).
}
\end{equation}

A positive maximum indicates a safety violation.

\paragraph{Counterexample Feedback.}
Starting with $\mathcal S_0=\{0\}$, each discovered
violating disturbance is retained:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal S_{j+1}=\mathcal S_j\cup\{b_j^*\}.
}
\end{equation}

The next optimization introduces one state trajectory
per retained disturbance, with shared controls:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\bm u,\{\bm{x}_b\}}\quad
&\int_0^T v(t)\,dt\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
&\dot{\bm{x}}_b=\bm f_b(\bm{x}_b,\bm u),
&&b\in\mathcal S_{j+1},\\
&q(\bm{x}_b(t))\leq0,
&&\forall t,\ b\in\mathcal S_{j+1},\\
&\bm G(\bm u(t))\leq\bm0,
&&\forall t\in[0,T],\\
&\bm{x}_b(0)=\bm{x}_{\mathrm{init}},
&&b\in\mathcal S_{j+1},\\
&\bm{x}_0(T)=\bm{x}_{\mathrm{goal}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Integration into SCvx.}
For each scenario, SCvx linearizes the disturbed
dynamics around a reference trajectory and applies
the affine obstacle-avoidance restriction:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
q(\bar{\bm p}_b)
-2(\bar{\bm p}_b-\bm c)^\top
(\bm p_b-\bar{\bm p}_b)\leq0.
}
\end{equation}

This is a conservative convex restriction since
$q(\bm p_b)$ equals the affine expression minus
$\|\bm p_b-\bar{\bm p}_b\|_2^2$.

Continuous-time safety constraints can be handled
using OpenSCvx's existing framework.

The outer loop alternates falsification and SCvx
re-optimization, adding scenarios only when a
violation is discovered.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Only nominal terminal-state feasibility is imposed.
Safety over the full uncertainty set requires
certified falsification or additional verification.
Convergence and computational advantages of the
combined algorithm remain to be established.

\section{Development and Problem Solution}
OpenSCvx solves nonlinear optimal control problems by iteratively writing and
solving convex subproblems around a nominal trajectory. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A planar unicycle moves over a fixed \(8\,\mathrm{s}\) horizon.
    \item The vehicle starts at \((0,0,0)\) and must reach \((4,0,0)\).
    \item The controls satisfy \(0\le v\le1\,\mathrm{m/s}\) and \(|\omega|\le0.8\,\mathrm{rad/s}\).
    \item A circular obstacle centered at \((2,0)\) has radius \(0.55\,\mathrm{m}\).
    \item The path must satisfy \(\|\bm{p}(t)-(2,0)^\top\|_2\ge0.55\,\mathrm{m}\) for all \(t\in[0,8]\).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Initial Experimental Results}
The recorded experiment uses OpenSCvx 0.5.2 and the original $N=5$
settings. A constant steering bias is searched over $[-0.05,0.05]$ rad/s
using 101 values. Final validation uses 501 bias values and 8001 time
samples per rollout, with independent SciPy integration. The retained
scenario set after one re-optimization is $\{0,-0.05\}$ rad/s.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\hline
Metric & Before & After\\
\hline
Worst sampled clearance (m) & $-0.131209$ & $-0.015776$\\
Geometric path length (m) & $4.181564$ & $4.257311$\\
Signed distance objective (m) & $4.170101$ & $4.240522$\\
Maximum applied speed (m/s) & $1.065051$ & $1.015535$\\
Minimum applied speed (m/s) & $-0.088544$ & $-0.108607$\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both SCvx solves reported convergence. The final nominal trajectory has
sampled clearance $0.083289$ m, but the retained disturbed scenario still
collides. The run therefore demonstrates scenario feedback and improved
clearance, not a successful robust repair. Total runtime was approximately
73 s, including approximately 37 s for independent falsification and
validation and 2 s in the optimizer loops. Remaining time includes
initialization, compilation, post-processing, and figure generation.

Inspection of the installed interpolation found
$\beta=N(\tau-\tau_k)$ over a segment of width $1/(N-1)$.
Thus $\beta$ reaches 1.25 for $N=5$, and the applied controls extrapolate
beyond the next knot. This causes between-node speed-bound violations;
the dense \texttt{u\_full} samples use a different interpolation.
Independent integration of the reconstructed applied controls reproduces
OpenSCvx's propagated pose within approximately $10^{-5}$.
Geometric length uses $\int |v|\,dt$, while the original objective uses
$\int v\,dt$; these differ because applied speed becomes negative.
These implementation and numerical limitations must be resolved before
claiming physical feasibility or robustness. The baseline also has a small
zero-bias collision. Finite-grid validation is not a continuous-uncertainty
safety certificate.

\section{Conclusions}
One complete falsification, counterexample feedback, and OpenSCvx re-solve
cycle improved sampled obstacle clearance but retained a collision and
applied control-bound violations. The experiment does not establish robust
feasibility, algorithmic novelty, or comparative computational advantages.
The next step is to resolve the diagnosed interpolation and constraint
accuracy issues before broader robustness experiments.
